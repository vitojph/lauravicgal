      Danay was born in Bauta, near Havana, Cuba. One of her most vivid
      childhood memories is hiding among the trees on her grandfather’s farm,
      climbing a guava tree where she could watch the road beyond a freshly
      plowed field without being seen. It was her own small, secluded world,
      surrounded by nature and the promise of an open horizon.




      Throughout her life, Danay has moved from one country to another. Each time, she unpacks, puts down roots, and slowly learns to call a place home. Yet just as those roots grow deeper, she must pack again and move on. She compares this constant journey to that of Bedouins crossing the desert, leaving behind years of life like grains of sand scattered across the landscape.




      Today, Danay hopes to transform those grains of sand into something greater, building something meaningful from her journey. Open landscapes- the desert, the sea along Havana’s Malecón, or the vastness of a field- represent her desire to move from a small, enclosed world toward something larger, from introspection toward freedom. She also imagines a landscape of interconnected paths, like the branches of neurons, reflecting how people reach out to one another across distances and borders.




      Above all, Danay wants others to feel that they belong and that they are not alone. For her, belonging is not about being confined to one place, but about finding the freedom to grow, connect, and become part of something greater. Her journey is a search for that open space where individual lives meet, and where every grain of sand has the potential to become something extraordinary.
