What happens when moving to a new country changes not only your surroundings, but also the way you see yourself and others?

Anastasiia was born in Moscow, Russia, where her professional life was closely connected to the Ministry of Agriculture. She remembers carrying wheat-shaped brooches, a small but meaningful symbol of the work and identity that shaped one chapter of her life.

Her story is also about the expectations placed on women.

In Moscow, beauty, elegance, and being perfectly dressed were often part of how a woman was expected to present herself. But life in a new country has given Anastasiia the freedom to look at herself and the world differently.

Immigration has changed the way she understands people.

Today, she feels more open and kind toward others. She has learned that everyone makes mistakes, and that starting over is not easy. Rather than judging, she believes in supporting one another. Sometimes, a hug can say enough.

### Finding a new sense of belonging

In New York, Anastasiia has discovered something she did not experience in quite the same way before: being surrounded by people from all over the world.

Books, people, and community have gradually become new symbols of who she is.

Blue and purple remain colors that follow her, while the ocean represents something still open and unknown.

And perhaps that is one of the most beautiful parts of her journey.

When asked where she sees herself in the future, Anastasiia admits that she doesn't really know yet.

*"Hopefully I will be helpful. I don't know where."*

There is something powerful in that uncertainty. Not knowing exactly where you are going does not mean you are lost. Sometimes, it simply means that your story is still being written.

### Between two lives

For ImmigrART, I wanted to capture the space between what was and what is becoming.

Anastasiia carries two lives, not as two separate worlds, but as two chapters that continue to shape each other.

Her past has not disappeared. The wheat-shaped brooches speak of the life she once knew. The books, the people, and the community around her speak of her present. And the ocean looks toward a future that has not yet been defined.

Between two lives, Anastasiia is finding her own way.

Not perfection.
Not certainty.

Just something much simpler: **peace of mind, support, and the freedom to become whoever she wants to be.**

This is Anastasiia's story.

And this is ImmigrART: one immigrant, one story, one visual journey at a time.
